# Interview Prep — Qualia, Engineering Manager, Information Security

## Likely behavioral questions
1. Tell me about a time you directed priorities for a team without holding a formal management title over them —
   how did you decide what got worked on first, and how did you get buy-in? (Draw on directing Treasury SOC's
   sprint priorities.)
2. Walk through a time you built automation that changed how a team worked, not just your own workflow.
3. Describe a security incident you supported — what was your role, and what would you have needed to actually
   run point as incident commander?
4. Tell me about a time you had to communicate a technical risk to a non-technical stakeholder or executive.
5. Describe a time you had to balance "the safe path to yes" against a reflexive "no" on a security question.
6. Tell me about mentoring or growing a less experienced teammate, even informally.
7. Describe a time you owned something end to end, from a ground-up build, with minimal existing structure
   (DOE/NNSA platform build is a strong example here).
8. How do you approach evaluating the security implications of a new AI/LLM tool the business wants to adopt?

## Likely technical questions
1. Be ready to speak directly to the gap: "Have you formally managed a security engineering or SecOps team?"
   Answer honestly — team-lead/sprint-priority-direction experience on Treasury's SOC, not a formal management
   title or headcount ownership. Frame it as leadership readiness, not equivalent experience.
2. Walk through the GenAI/automation tooling you've built for detection and triage — be ready to map this
   directly onto Qualia's stated goal of making AI-assisted workflows the default across detection, triage,
   investigation, and response.
3. Describe your experience with SIEM/log monitoring platforms and how you'd approach reviewing/tuning
   detection logic at a company Qualia's size.
4. What's your experience with SOC 2 or ISO 27001 control ownership? Be honest this isn't a confirmed area —
   describe adjacent experience evaluating security controls/products broadly.
5. How would you approach the escalation path for a customer-reported security concern (e.g., suspected fraud
   or account compromise)? This is a direct JD responsibility Kyle hasn't owned before — think through the
   logic in advance rather than improvising in the room.
6. Familiarity with IAM concepts (SSO, MFA, RBAC) — be ready to speak to hands-on AWS/GCP IAM policy/role work
   and multi-SIEM API token/permission management as the concrete evidence.
7. What offensive security exposure do you have? Answer honestly this isn't a confirmed area of depth.

## Questions to ask them
1. What does the path from "player" to "coach" look like in this role day to day — how much of the job is
   hands-on detection/incident work vs. team management in practice?
2. What's the team's current headcount and hiring plan, and what would my first 90 days of team-building
   priorities look like?
3. How is "risk actually going down" measured at Qualia — what metrics does the security team report against?
4. Given the role is based in Austin with some openness to remote, what does that flexibility look like in
   practice for someone based in the Dallas/Ft. Worth area?
5. How does the security team currently use AI-assisted workflows, and what's the biggest gap between where
   that is today and where you want it to be?

## Salary anchor
Posted band is $130,887.07–$189,743.63. Bottom is below Kyle's $170K floor. Given the real gap on formal
people-management experience, this is not a role to anchor at the very top of the band by default — but the
strong automation/AI-differentiator fit and detection engineering depth support anchoring toward the upper
third if the conversation gets that far. Confirm remote-eligibility and realistic comp range early, since the
JD's "open to exploring remote possibilities" language is non-committal.

## Closing script
"The player/coach structure of this role is exactly the kind of stretch I'm looking for — I've been directing
priorities for a live SOC's detection backlog without holding the formal title, and the automation-first
mandate you're describing is what I've spent the last two years building in practice. I'd want to talk through
what formal team-leadership ramp-up looks like in the first few months. What are the next steps from here?"
