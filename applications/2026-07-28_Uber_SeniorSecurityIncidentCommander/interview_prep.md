# Interview Prep — Uber, Senior Security Incident Commander

## Likely behavioral questions
1. Tell me about the most complex or ambiguous technical investigation you've led — walk through it end to end.
2. Describe a time you had to communicate a fast-moving technical situation to a non-technical audience.
3. Tell me about a time you had to make a high-consequence call with incomplete information.
4. Describe a time you mentored someone through a difficult technical investigation.
5. Walk through how you prioritize when multiple things demand your attention simultaneously (as in an active incident).

## Likely technical questions
1. Walk through your process for investigating anomalous entity behavior — how do you go from a raw signal to a validated root cause?
2. How would your MITRE ATT&CK-aligned detection content translate into supporting a live incident-command process, versus the alerting role it plays today?
3. This role wants someone who can be "the incident authority, not just a facilitator" — how do you distinguish between coordinating a response and directing one technically?
4. Describe how you'd use AI-assisted tooling (like what you've built) for correlation and decision support during a live, high-severity incident.
5. You haven't run formal tabletop exercises or purple-team simulations — how would you approach designing one from scratch?
6. Walk through how you'd brief an executive on a fast-moving incident when the technical picture is still incomplete.

## Questions to ask them
1. What does the on-call rotation actually look like in practice — frequency, escalation path, and expected response time?
2. How does this role currently interface with legal, privacy, and other cross-functional teams during an active incident?
3. What's the split between hands-on technical investigation and pure incident-command/coordination in a typical week?
4. How mature is the tabletop/simulation program today, and what would this hire be expected to build versus inherit?
5. What does the transition look like for someone strong on detection engineering and technical investigation, newer to formal incident-command authority and executive briefing?

## Salary anchor
Posted range: **$180,000–$200,000/year** base (SF/Seattle/Sunnyvale), plus bonus/equity — the lowest of the three Uber roles built today, though still comfortably above the $170K floor. Given the real scope gap (formal incident command and executive communication are not yet confirmed experience), anchor initial expectations to the lower half of the band and let the interview process surface whether the role is more detection-engineering-adjacent than the title implies.

## Closing-the-interview script
"I'd love to bring that technical investigation and MITRE ATT&CK-aligned detection background to incident command — what would the ramp look like for someone strong on the technical/investigative side, growing into the formal command and executive-briefing parts of the role?"
