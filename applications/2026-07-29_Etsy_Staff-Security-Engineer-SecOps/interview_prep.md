# Interview Prep — Etsy, Staff Security Engineer I, Security Operations

## Likely behavioral questions
1. Tell me about a time you led an incident from detection through resolution and communicated it to leadership.
2. Describe a time you coached or paired with a more junior engineer through a post-incident review.
3. Tell me about a manual process you identified and automated — what was the impact?
4. How do you decide when to trust an AI-assisted output versus verify it manually?
5. Describe a time you had to reason about blast radius or data exposure in real time during an active incident.

## Likely technical questions
1. Walk through your nine-SIEM orchestration framework and how you validated detection quality before rollout.
2. How have you built or evaluated AI agents for security workflows, and what's your process for trusting their output?
3. Describe your approach to threat modeling for a web application or cloud data flow.
4. What's your experience with malware behavior/persistence mechanisms specifically? (Be honest: this is a real gap — Kyle's depth is in detection content and telemetry, not host-level forensics/malware reverse engineering.)
5. How do you build detection rules and threat-hunting queries from scratch versus tuning existing ones?
6. Walk through your CI/CD pipeline for detection-as-code — what does "automated test" mean in that context?

## Sharp questions to ask them
1. How is the balance today between manual detection engineering and the AI-assisted automation this role is chartered to expand?
2. What does the on-call rotation look like in practice, and how does the team handle handoffs across time zones?
3. How does this team measure success — MTTD, coverage, or something tied to specific incident outcomes?
4. What's the current relationship between Security Operations and the broader engineering org when it comes to driving fixes?
5. Given the "Staff Security Engineer I" title, what's the growth path from here?

## Salary anchor
Posted range: $204,000–$240,000. Anchor to the top third (~$230K+) given strong direct overlap on the AI-assisted automation ask specifically.

## Honest gap to address directly if asked
OS internals, malware functionality, and persistence mechanisms are listed as qualities that help someone thrive in this role — Kyle's depth is in detection content, telemetry pipelines, and automation, not host-level forensics or malware reverse engineering. Be upfront about this if it comes up rather than implying deeper malware-analysis experience than exists.

## Closing-the-interview script
"The AI-assisted automation piece is exactly the kind of work I've been doing for the last few years, and I think that's the strongest match here. Where I'd want to be upfront is the host-level malware/persistence side — that's not where my depth is, though the detection-content and telemetry work overlaps heavily with what that requires. What would help you evaluate whether that gap matters for this specific role?"
