# Interview Prep — Netflix, Security Engineer (L5), Workforce Security

Note: this is your lowest-fit application of the three (real gaps on MDM/hardening/patch ownership). Prep leans on framing your genuine data/analytics differentiator confidently while being upfront about the gaps rather than getting caught flat-footed on them.

## Likely behavioral questions
1. Tell me about a time you had to learn a new domain quickly to be effective.
2. Describe translating a complex technical risk into a business decision for non-technical stakeholders.
3. Tell me about evaluating a vendor security tool or control — how did you decide if it was worth adopting?
4. Describe operating autonomously on an open-ended problem with no clear owner.
5. Tell me about a time data-quality issues masked a real security risk — how did you catch it?
6. Describe partnering cross-functionally (e.g., SOC stakeholders) to get a detection or process adopted.

## Likely technical questions
1. Walk through how you'd build a prioritization pipeline on top of raw Tenable vulnerability-scan output — what signals would you weight?
2. You haven't owned host-hardening before — how would you approach designing a Mac/Windows/Linux baseline, and what's your learning plan?
3. Walk through a specific example of evaluating an environment or system for vulnerability exposure.
4. What would the analytics/reporting layer on top of an existing vulnerability scanner look like if you built it from scratch?
5. Without formal risk-scoring frameworks, how have you informally decided which of many security controls/vendors to prioritize?
6. How would you use GenAI to accelerate patch/vulnerability triage, given your existing GenAI tooling background?

Be direct if asked point-blank about MDM/hardening/patch ownership: name the gap the same way the cover letter does, then pivot to the data/analytics angle you'd bring instead.

## Questions to ask them
1. Given the role centers on host hardening and patch management, which isn't my direct background — what does ramp-up typically look like for someone strong on the data/analytics side but newer to endpoint hardening specifically?
2. How does the Workforce Security team currently prioritize vulnerability remediation — is there an existing scoring/analytics layer, or is that greenfield?
3. What does the MDM stack look like today (Jamf/Kandji/Intune), and how much of the role is policy design vs. day-to-day administration?
4. How does the team measure success for host hardening and configuration-drift prevention?
5. Given the very wide comp band, what factors most influence where an offer lands within it?

## Salary anchor
Posted range: **$400,000–$680,000** (all salary/stock choice, no bonus — Netflix's standard model). This clears your $170K floor many times over. Given the real domain gaps, this is a case for a candid leveling/comp conversation rather than assuming top-of-band by default — but there's still room to anchor toward the upper-middle of the range if the panel confirms genuine interest despite the gaps.

## Closing-the-interview script
"I want to be upfront that hardening and patch-management ownership aren't where my background is strongest — but the data and analytics discipline I'd bring to vulnerability prioritization is real and differentiated. I'd love to hear how the team thinks about that trade-off, and what next steps look like."
