# Interview Prep — HackerOne, Manager, Data Engineering

## Likely behavioral questions
1. Tell me about a time you built a single source of truth from fragmented or inconsistent data — walk through
   the CIM build at Cysiv as your strongest example.
2. Describe a time you led priorities for a team without holding the formal management title (Treasury SOC
   sprint-priority direction).
3. Tell me about a time you had to learn a new tool stack quickly to stay effective on a project.
4. Walk through a time you presented data findings to a non-technical stakeholder — how did you adapt the story?
5. Describe operating in an early-stage, ambiguous environment where you had to define your own priorities.
6. Tell me about a data quality or reliability problem you caught and fixed before it affected downstream users.
7. Describe a time you mentored a less experienced engineer.

## Likely technical questions
1. Be ready to speak directly to the tool-stack gap: "What's your hands-on experience with Airflow, Snowflake,
   DBT, Meltano, or Fivetran?" Answer honestly — these are not confirmed skills; pivot to the adjacent tooling
   you do have deep experience with (GCP Dataproc/Dataflow, PySpark, Logstash) and your track record of ramping
   quickly on new platforms.
2. Walk through the Common Information Model build at Cysiv in detail — this is your strongest direct analog to
   "source of truth data marts" and should be your headline technical story.
3. How would you approach designing a data mart for a business function you're not deeply familiar with (e.g.,
   Sales/Salesforce data)? Be honest this is a specialization shift from your security-telemetry background —
   describe your general approach to schema design and stakeholder discovery instead.
4. What's your experience with Looker, Tableau, Sigma, Domo, or PowerBI specifically? Answer honestly (Kibana
   dashboarding is the closest confirmed analog, not these named BI tools).
5. Describe your approach to data pipeline reliability and monitoring at scale (220+ sources is a strong,
   concrete example here).
6. "At least 3+ years in a leadership role" is a stated minimum qualification — be ready to speak plainly about
   where your team-lead experience does and doesn't meet that bar, rather than letting it go unaddressed.

## Questions to ask them
1. Since the role is "targeted" for candidates near Boston, Austin, or DC but listed as Remote — what does that
   mean in practice for someone based outside those hub radii? How often is in-person collaboration expected?
2. What does "leadership role" mean concretely for this position on day one — team size, hiring authority,
   performance-review ownership?
3. What's the current state of the DataOne team's source-of-truth program — greenfield build or an existing
   foundation I'd be extending?
4. How is success measured for this role in the first 90 days beyond what's in the JD?
5. Given the modern-data-stack tooling (Airflow/Snowflake/DBT/Fivetran) is central to this role, what does
   onboarding/ramp-up look like for someone strong on the underlying data-engineering fundamentals but new to
   this specific stack?
