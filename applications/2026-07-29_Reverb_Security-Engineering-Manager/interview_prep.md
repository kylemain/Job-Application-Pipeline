# Interview Prep — Reverb, Security Engineering Manager

## Role snapshot
- Hybrid: Chicago office once/week (Tuesdays); local candidates preferred, but open to fully-remote US candidates "for the right fit" — worth confirming early
- Base salary: $171,000–$223,000 (Chicago-anchored band)
- Charter: lead security engineers/analysts; sprint planning; security reviews/audits/assessments; architecture feedback; GRC/policy advisory to engineering leadership.

## Likely behavioral questions
1. This role asks for 2+ years leading or supervising security teams — walk me through your closest experience to that, even without the formal title.
2. Tell me about a security sprint retrospective or planning cycle you ran or heavily influenced — what changed as a result?
3. Describe a time you had to advise engineering leadership on policy or process, not just a technical fix.
4. How have you balanced proactive/preventative security work against reactive incident response demands?
5. Tell me about a time you worked with compliance or regulatory requirements (PCI, SOX, or similar) — be honest if this is limited; focus instead on adjacent data-governance/access-control work.
6. Describe your experience (if any) with vulnerability management programs or bug bounty coordination.

## Likely technical questions
1. Walk through your GitLab CI/CD detection-as-code pipeline, including the automated tests you wrote for the pipeline itself.
2. What's your hands-on experience with Terraform, Helm, or Kubernetes specifically? (Be direct: GitLab CI/CD is the well-evidenced piece; Kubernetes exposure is platform-user-level, not administration; no confirmed Terraform/Helm ownership.)
3. How would you approach IAM/RBAC design using tools like Okta or AWS SSO, given your AWS/GCP IAM policy/role background?
4. Talk through your CrowdStrike and SentinelOne integration work as part of the nine-platform orchestration framework.
5. How do you think about detection and response tooling (SIEM/EDR/IDS/WAF) working together across an e-commerce platform's architecture?
6. What's your approach to security code/architecture reviews for a fast-moving product engineering org?

## Sharp questions to ask them
1. Is the hybrid/Chicago expectation flexible in practice, or is "open to remote for the right fit" more aspirational than real day-to-day?
2. What does the current security engineering team's split look like across detection/response, vuln management, and compliance work?
3. How much of this role is hands-on technical work versus pure people/program management day to day?
4. What's driving this hire — team growth, a gap left by departure, or a new mandate (e.g., compliance push, incident aftermath)?
5. How does the security team currently engage with GRC/regulatory work like PCI or SOX — is that owned by this role or a separate function?

## Salary anchor
Posted range is $171,000–$223,000. Anchor to the top third (~$210K+) once fit is confirmed — this clears Kyle's $170K+ floor but with less room than several other roles in this pipeline, so weigh the remote-status conversation carefully before over-investing in this one.

## Closing-the-interview script
"I don't have the formal 'managed a team for 2+ years' title, but I've been the person directing sprint priorities and technical direction for detection content a live SOC depends on — and I'm genuinely energized by the parts of this role that are compliance and process, not just detection engineering. What would help you feel confident about that gap?"
