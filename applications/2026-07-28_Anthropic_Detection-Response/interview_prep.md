# Interview Prep — Anthropic, Security Engineer, Detection & Response

## Likely behavioral questions
1. Tell me about a time you led an incident response effort end to end — what was your role, what went well, what would you change?
2. Describe a time you built or deployed a novel piece of tooling (possibly LLM-based) that meaningfully improved detection or response.
3. Tell me about a time you had to coordinate response efforts across multiple teams during an active incident.
4. Describe how you review incident-response metrics and drive continuous improvement.
5. Tell me about a time you had to pick up a new technology or platform quickly under pressure.
6. Describe a disagreement with another security or engineering team about incident scope or severity — how did you resolve it?

## Likely technical questions
1. Walk through your Python-based SIEM/EDR orchestration framework — architecture, API-level access management, and how you handled failure modes across nine platforms.
2. How have you used or would you use an LLM to enhance detection, investigation, or response specifically (not just generic AI-adjacent claims — be concrete)?
3. Explain your approach to creating and optimizing detections, playbooks, and workflows.
4. What's your experience with Kubernetes-based security operations? (Honest answer: you've worked on a platform that used Kubernetes for container orchestration, but didn't manage/administer the cluster yourself — real exposure to the platform, not cluster-admin or K8s security-ops ownership. Pivot to your rapid platform-learning track record.)
5. Walk through a specific detection you built using signature, behavioral, statistical, or ML-based techniques, and why you chose that approach.
6. How do you think about on-call incident response at a fast-moving, high-consequence company like Anthropic?

## Sharp questions to ask them
1. What does "frontline" mean concretely for this role — what's the current incident volume and severity mix?
2. How mature is the LLM-based tooling mentioned in the JD today — is this role building it from scratch or extending existing work?
3. How does the Detection & Response team partner with the research organization on frontier-model-specific risks?
4. What does the on-call rotation actually look like in practice — frequency, escalation support, etc.?
5. How does Anthropic think about the 25%-in-office policy for a Detection & Response role specifically — is there flexibility given the on-call nature of the work?

## Honest framing for the remote/hybrid gap
Kyle's entire recent career (since 2023) has been fully remote. Be upfront in the screen about currently being based in the Dallas/Ft. Worth area and ask directly what the realistic in-office cadence would look like for someone relocating their routine around 25% office time — better to surface this early than have it become a late-stage surprise.

## Salary anchor
Posted range $300K–$405K — second-highest in Kyle's current pipeline. Anchor toward the top third (~$375K–$405K) given the strength of the GenAI-tooling and multi-platform orchestration match, while being realistic that the hybrid requirement is a real lifestyle change worth factoring into total-comp expectations.

## Closing script
"Leading incident response and building the detections and playbooks a team runs on is what I've spent my career doing, and the direction this role is taking — using LLMs directly inside that workflow — is exactly where I've already been building production tooling. I'd love to talk through what the in-office cadence looks like in practice for this team."
